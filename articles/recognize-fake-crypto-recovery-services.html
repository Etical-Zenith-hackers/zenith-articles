How to Recognize Fake Cryptocurrency Recovery Services

After losing crypto, people can be targeted by a second fraud that promises to retrieve the first loss. A professional-looking website or convincing testimonial is not proof that a service is legitimate.

Guaranteed recovery claims

Blockchain tracing may identify transaction paths or leads, but it does not automatically reveal an owner or provide power to return funds. Promises of certain recovery, secret access, or guaranteed timelines are major warning signs.

Advance fees and invented charges

Be suspicious of demands for a release fee, tax, activation payment, gas deposit, or insurance charge paid to an unknown wallet. Verify any legitimate professional fee through a written contract and independent checks.

Requests for wallet secrets

Never disclose a seed phrase or private key. A legitimate investigator does not need your secret recovery phrase to analyze a public transaction hash. Never sign an unfamiliar transaction just because someone says it will recover assets.

Fake authority and impersonation

Scammers may pretend to be police, regulators, exchanges, lawyers, or blockchain analysts. Find the organization’s official contact details independently and verify registrations or credentials through the relevant official directory.

Pressure and secrecy

Urgency, threats, demands to keep the matter secret, and requests to install remote-control software are reasons to stop and verify.

Safer due diligence

  • Search for independent information and complaints, not only testimonials on the provider's own site.
  • Request written scope, deliverables, privacy terms, and fee schedule.
  • Do not give a provider control of your accounts or assets.
