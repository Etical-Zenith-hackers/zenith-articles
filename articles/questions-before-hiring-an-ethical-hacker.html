Questions to Ask Before Hiring an Ethical Hacker

A short, structured interview can help you distinguish a responsible security professional from an unsuitable or risky provider. Ask for clear answers before granting access or signing an agreement.

What exactly will you test?

Ask the provider to identify each domain, IP range, application, cloud tenant, or device included in the engagement. Confirm exclusions and how any third-party services will be handled. Avoid vague permission such as “test everything.”

What written authorization is required?

Get a signed agreement from someone with authority to approve the work. It should identify the systems, dates, allowed techniques, contacts, and stop conditions. Confirm how the provider will validate that the scope has not changed.

How will you protect data?

  • Ask who can access test data and evidence.
  • Confirm encryption, storage location, retention period, and secure deletion process.
  • Agree how personal or regulated information will be minimized and reported.

How are findings validated and prioritized?

Ask for a sample redacted report and how severity is assigned. Good reporting explains impact, supporting evidence, limitations, and practical remediation instead of relying on dramatic claims.

What happens after testing?

Confirm whether a briefing, remediation support, and retesting are included. Ask how urgent findings are escalated and whether the final report separates confirmed vulnerabilities from observations or hypotheses.

What are warning signs?

Be cautious if a provider refuses a contract, proposes unauthorized access, guarantees that all vulnerabilities will be found, or pressures you to disclose credentials before the engagement is verified.
