How to Protect a Website Admin Account

A compromised administrator account can expose customer information, alter content, or disrupt a website. Protect admin access as carefully as other high-value business accounts.

Use unique credentials and MFA

Use a long, unique password and enable multifactor authentication for hosting, CMS, domain registrar, email, and source-control accounts.

Apply least privilege

Give each person only the access needed for their role. Remove old accounts and revoke tokens when staff or contractors no longer need access.

Keep software and integrations current

Patch the CMS, themes, plugins, dependencies, and deployment tools. Remove unused components and review third-party integrations before granting access.

Protect backups and secrets

Keep tested backups separate from the production environment. Store API keys and deployment secrets in approved secret storage, not public repositories or frontend code.

Monitor changes and alerts

Enable login and deployment notifications where available. Review unexpected administrator accounts, content changes, DNS edits, and unfamiliar access tokens.

Prepare recovery steps

Document who controls the domain, hosting, source repository, and account recovery methods. Test restoration procedures and keep emergency contacts current.
