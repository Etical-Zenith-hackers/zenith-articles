How to Recognize a Fake Crypto Investment Platform

A polished website or dashboard is not proof that an investment service holds real assets. Fraudulent platforms may display invented profits and demand additional payments when a customer tries to withdraw.

Verify the operator independently

Check the legal entity, address, regulatory status where applicable, and official contact details through independent registries. A logo or copied registration number is not enough.

Be cautious of guaranteed returns

Crypto prices are volatile. Claims of guaranteed high profits, secret algorithms, or no-risk trading deserve skepticism, especially when paired with urgency or referral pressure.

Treat displayed balances carefully

A number on a website may be only a database entry. Do not assume that a visible balance proves funds are held or can be withdrawn.

Watch for withdrawal barriers

Demands for extra taxes, insurance, activation payments, or release fees sent to an unfamiliar wallet are serious warning signs. Contact a bank, exchange, or authority using verified details instead.

Preserve the evidence

Save the full domain, account statements, messages, payment receipts, wallet addresses, transaction hashes, and a timeline of deposits and withdrawal attempts.

Report and protect remaining funds

Stop sending money, notify relevant financial services, and report the incident to appropriate authorities. Beware of recovery agents who promise guaranteed refunds.
