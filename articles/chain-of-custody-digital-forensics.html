What Is a Chain of Custody in Digital Forensics?

A chain of custody is a record of how evidence is identified, handled, transferred, stored, and accessed. The appropriate process depends on the evidence and the purpose of the investigation.

Identify each item

Assign a unique identifier and describe the device, file, account export, or other evidence clearly enough to distinguish it from other items.

Record collection details

Document who collected the item, when and where it was collected, the authority for collection, and the method used.

Track every transfer

Record the person transferring the evidence, the recipient, date and time, purpose, and relevant packaging or storage details.

Control access and storage

Restrict access to authorized people and use appropriate physical or digital safeguards. Record examination, copying, and export actions.

Document integrity checks

Where appropriate, use validated procedures and cryptographic hashes to help detect changes to files or forensic images. A hash alone does not establish that the original collection was lawful or complete.

Match the process to the case

Follow organizational policies and relevant legal requirements. For evidence likely to be used in court, seek qualified forensic and legal guidance.
