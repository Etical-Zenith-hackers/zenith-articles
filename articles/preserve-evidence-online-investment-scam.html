How to Preserve Evidence After an Online Investment Scam

When an investment opportunity may be fraudulent, preserving a clear record can help you explain what happened to a bank, platform, lawyer, or authority. Do not continue sending money to test a promise.

Save the original communications

Keep emails, chat exports, messages, attachments, usernames, phone numbers, profile URLs, and dates. Avoid editing the original files; create working copies if annotations are needed.

Record all payments

List the date, amount, currency, payment method, recipient details, transaction references, and related wallet addresses. Include bank transfers, cards, wire payments, and crypto transfers.

Document the platform

Save the full URL, company claims, account statements, withdrawal messages, and visible balances. A displayed balance on a website may not represent assets actually held for you.

Build a timeline

Write down when you first encountered the offer, who contacted you, what was promised, when payments were made, when withdrawals failed, and what explanations were given. Mark uncertain dates as estimates.

Contact financial providers and authorities

Use verified contact details to notify banks, payment providers, exchanges, and the appropriate reporting authorities. Ask what evidence they need and keep case numbers.

Protect yourself from follow-up scams

Do not pay extra “tax” or “release” charges to withdraw supposed profits. Be cautious of recovery firms that guarantee a result or request account secrets.
