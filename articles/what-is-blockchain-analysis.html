What Is Blockchain Analysis?

Blockchain analysis examines activity recorded on a blockchain to understand transaction flows and develop investigative leads. It is useful in some cases, but public data has important limits.

What a public ledger can show

Depending on the network, an explorer may show transaction identifiers, addresses, token movements, block times, and values. These records can help establish a transaction timeline.

What it cannot prove alone

An address is not automatically a named person. A transaction pattern may support a hypothesis but does not by itself prove ownership, intent, or a legal conclusion. Off-chain records may be needed.

How analysts organize a case

A careful review records the chain, transaction hash, address, timestamp, amount, source of each observation, and unresolved questions. Analysts distinguish verified facts from inferences.

Privacy and attribution limits

Wallet ownership may be obscured by shared services, custodial platforms, mixers, cross-chain bridges, or incomplete public information. Attribution should be stated cautiously and supported by corroboration.

Preserve evidence responsibly

Keep original transaction identifiers and relevant communications, note the source and collection date, and avoid altering evidence. Follow applicable law and platform reporting procedures.

No automatic recovery

Tracing may produce leads but does not itself freeze, seize, or return funds. Any action against assets depends on platform cooperation, legal authority, jurisdiction, and the facts.
