How to Respond to a Phishing Email at Work

A quick, calm response can reduce the impact of a phishing attempt. Follow your organization's incident process and avoid forwarding suspicious attachments broadly.

Do not interact further

Avoid clicking links, opening attachments, replying, or entering credentials. If you already interacted, report that fact promptly rather than trying to hide it.

Use the approved reporting channel

Use your company's phishing-report button or security contact. Include the original message when possible so headers and metadata remain available.

If you entered a password

From a trusted device, follow the organization's credential-reset process, revoke sessions if instructed, and report any MFA prompts you did not initiate.

If a file ran or a device behaved strangely

Contact IT or security immediately. Follow instructions about disconnecting the device; avoid wiping it or deleting files if investigation may be required.

Preserve relevant details

Record the time, sender, URL, attachment name, actions taken, and any alerts. Do not upload confidential company data to unapproved services.

Improve prevention

Use unique credentials, MFA, current software, and clear reporting procedures. Training should encourage rapid reporting without blame.
